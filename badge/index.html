Build Your Badge

Your badge arrives part-built. Seven pieces are yours to solder — here's how to do it on your own, at your own pace, without queuing at the Soldering Village.

What's in your kit

Everything else — the microcontroller, the colour-changing LEDs, all the tiny parts — is already soldered on when you pick the badge up.

4×LEDgoes on the front
1×SAO connectorgoes on the front
1×Power switchgoes on the back
1×Battery holdergoes on the back
1×AAA batteryno soldering

A suggested order

Not a rule, just the order that keeps the board flattest and easiest to hold while you work — lowest parts first, tallest last.

  1. Power switch — back

    The only surface-mount piece, and the fiddliest. Do it while the board is still completely flat. Tack one corner pad, check the switch sits square against the printing, then solder the rest.

  2. The four LEDs — front

    Push them in from the front so the bodies stick out the art side, then turn the badge over and solder the legs on the back. Trim the legs afterwards.

  3. SAO connector — front

    Also fits from the front and solders from behind, so add-ons plug into the art side.

  4. Battery holder — back

    Tallest part, so it goes last. Sits on the back, soldered from the front.

  5. Battery in, switch to ON

    Drop the AAA into the holder and slide the switch. That's it.

Three things worth slowing down for

The SAO notch faces up

The six-pin socket only works one way round — notch toward the top edge of the badge. Fitted backwards, anything you plug into it gets power and ground swapped. It's printed on the back of the board if you want to double-check.

Square pad takes the short leg

Each LED has one square pad and one round one. The square pad takes the shorter leg — the one on the flat side of the LED body. Backwards, the LED simply never lights. No harm done, but you'll be desoldering it to turn it round.

The switch will test your patience

Six small pads and nothing holding it still. If you only ask for help with one thing, make it this one — the Soldering Village has irons and people all day, and nobody minds.

The back of the board

Every part is labelled on the back, and the fitting instructions are printed there too. Keep this side facing you while you work.

If it doesn't light up

In roughly the order worth checking.

Check the switch first

Genuinely the most common cause. Slide it to ON before you touch anything with an iron.

Then the battery

Line the holder up with the + printed on the board. If it did go in backwards the badge simply won't turn on — the board is built to survive that, so nothing is damaged.

Then the LED legs

An LED that stays dark while the others work is almost always the short leg in the round pad instead of the square one.

Still stuck? Bring it to the Soldering Village. Someone will happily take a look, whether or not you built it there.
